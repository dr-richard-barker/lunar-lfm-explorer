Comparative Planetary Magnetobiology of the Moon and Mars

Integrated Geophysical Mapping, Hypomagnetic Stress Synthesis, and Operational Life Support Frameworks for Deep-Space Exploration

Executive Abstract

Terrestrial life evolved over four billion years within the continuous, protective presence of Earth's geomagnetic field (GMF; ~50 µT). As space agencies prepare for sustained human presence on the Moon (Artemis program) and Mars, biological systems—including crops in bioregenerative life support systems (BLSS), closed-loop microbiomes, and human crew—will be permanently uprooted from this evolutionary baseline into severe hypomagnetic field (HMF) environments.

In this study, we present an integrated planetary magnetobiology investigation combining calibrated orbital and surface magnetometer datasets across 36 candidate, historical, and contemporary landing sites on the Moon (23 sites) and Mars (13 sites). We show that all 13 Artemis III candidate South Polar landing regions on the Moon reside within an ultra-low, near-null crustal magnetic baseline (|B30km| < 1.0 nT, >50,000× to >125,000× weaker than Earth). On Mars, we resolve a profound hemispheric dichotomy: ice-rich northern lowland candidate habitat sites (Arcadia Planitia, Deuteronilus Mensae) experience extreme near-null hypomagnetism (|Bsurf| < 25 nT), whereas southern highland anomaly belts (Terra Sirenum) harbor intense remnant crustal fields (|Bsurf| > 1500 nT) forming mini-magnetospheres. InSight magnetometry confirms ground-level bedrock amplification (~2013 nT).

Key Scientific Discoveries

🌕 The Lunar Polar Null Regime

All 13 candidate Artemis III South Polar landing sites experience fields <1.0 nT at 30 km altitude. This near-null baseline is 50,000× to 125,000× weaker than Earth's geomagnetic field, imposing chronic hypomagnetic stress on biological payloads.

🔴 Martian Hemispheric Dichotomy

Mars presents two divergent magnetic regimes: demagnetized northern lowlands (Arcadia ISRU candidate sites <20 nT) vs. magnetized southern highlands (Terra Sirenum >1500 nT mini-magnetospheres capable of plasma deflection).

🌱 Biophysical Stress Mechanisms

HMF exposure disrupts cryptochrome/phytochrome light sensing, PIN2 polar auxin transport, iron homeostasis, and thylakoid ultrastructure in plants, while accelerating osteoclastic bone loss in mammalian models.

⚡ Active Helmholtz Mitigation

We propose active magnetic restoration arrays (~50 µT artificial GMF) inside extraterrestrial growth chambers and standardized fluxgate magnetometers on precursor landers.
