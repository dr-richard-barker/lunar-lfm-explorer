\abstracttext{
Terrestrial life evolved over four billion years within the continuous, protective presence of Earth's geomagnetic field (GMF; $\sim\SI{50}{\micro\tesla}$). As international space agencies and commercial ventures prepare for sustained human presence on the Moon (Artemis program) and Mars, biological systems—including crops in bioregenerative life support systems (BLSS), closed-loop microbiomes, and human crew—will be permanently uprooted from this evolutionary baseline and deployed into severe hypomagnetic field (HMF) environments. In this study, we present an integrated planetary magnetobiology investigation combining calibrated orbital and surface magnetometer datasets across 36 candidate, historical, and contemporary landing sites on the Moon (23 sites) and Mars (13 sites) with a comprehensive synthesis of terrestrial magnetobiology literature. We show that all 13 Artemis III candidate South Polar landing regions on the Moon reside within an ultra-low, near-null crustal magnetic baseline ($|\mathbf{B}_{\text{30km}}| < \SI{1.0}{\nano\tesla}$, $>50,000\times$ to $>125,000\times$ weaker than Earth). On Mars, we resolve a profound hemispheric dichotomy: ice-rich northern lowland candidate habitat sites (Arcadia Planitia, Deuteronilus Mensae) and rover sites (Jezero Crater, Oxia Planum) experience extreme near-null hypomagnetism ($|\mathbf{B}_{\text{surf}}| < \SI{25}{\nano\tesla}$), whereas southern highland anomaly belts (Terra Sirenum) harbor intense remnant crustal fields ($|\mathbf{B}_{\text{surf}}| > \SI{1500}{\nano\tesla}$) capable of forming mini-magnetospheres. InSight magnetometry provides ground truth confirming local subsurface amplification ($\sim\SI{2013}{\nano\tesla}$). We synthesize empirical biological evidence demonstrating that HMF exposure disrupts cryptochrome- and phytochrome-mediated light sensing, PIN2 polar auxin transport, iron homeostasis, and thylakoid ultrastructure in plants, while inducing circadian arrhythmicity, altered DNA repair spin kinetics, and accelerated osteoclastic bone demineralization in mammalian systems. Finally, we formulate a multi-planetary biological risk matrix and establish operational recommendations, emphasizing active Helmholtz coil magnetic restoration ($\sim\SI{50}{\micro\tesla}$) in surface growth modules and standardized in situ magnetometry on precursor landers.
}
